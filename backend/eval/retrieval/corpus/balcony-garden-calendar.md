# Balcony garden calendar

A month by month plan for the tomatoes, the herbs and the strawberries on the south balcony.

## February

Order seeds and compost. This year: the tomato Red Ember, which stays compact enough for pots, basil, flat-leaf parsley and a tray of cut-and-come-again lettuce. Clean the pots with hot soapy water.

## March

Sow the tomatoes indoors in the middle of the month, two seeds to a small pot, on the kitchen windowsill. They need about 20 °C to come up, which takes a week to ten days. Thin to the strongest seedling once they have their first true leaves.

## April

Move the seedlings on into 9 cm pots when their roots show at the bottom. From the middle of the month, put them outside on warm days and bring them in at night, so they toughen up slowly.

## May

Plant them out for good once the last frost has passed, usually around 10 May here. Each plant goes into a 30 litre pot with a stake, and the strawberries get fresh compost in their trough. Sow basil straight into its pot now that nights stay above 10 °C.

## June

Feed the tomatoes every week with a high-potash feed from the moment the first truss has set its fruit. Pinch out the side shoots that grow between the main stem and the leaves, and tie the stem to its stake every 20 cm.

## July and August

Water in the evening, never in the midday sun, and water the soil rather than the leaves; a pot this size can need 3 litres on a hot day. Pick basil from the top so it bushes out, and cut off any flower spikes. Watch for blight after warm, wet spells: brown patches on the leaves that spread fast. Take off affected leaves at once and bin them, not into the compost.

## September

Pick the last of the fruit before the nights turn cold. Green tomatoes ripen indoors in a drawer with a banana. Empty the pots onto the compost heap, and keep the stakes.

## Pests

Aphids on the basil come off with a spray of soapy water, every three days for two weeks. Yellow sticky traps hung between the tomatoes catch whitefly. Slugs rarely reach a balcony this high, but a copper ring around the lettuce pot stops the few that do.
