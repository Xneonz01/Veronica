# Family recipes

## Breads

### Focaccia

500 g strong white flour, 400 ml warm water, 10 g salt, 7 g dried yeast and 60 ml olive oil, plus flaky salt and rosemary for the top. Use a 30 by 40 cm tin with a lip, oiled well in the corners, because the dough sticks there first.

Mix everything except the oil into a wet, shaggy dough and leave it covered for 30 minutes. Stretch and fold it four times, 20 minutes apart, then pour half the oil into the tin, turn the dough into it and leave it to prove for an hour until it has nearly doubled and is full of bubbles.

Pour over the rest of the oil and press deep dimples into the dough with oiled fingers, right down to the tin. Scatter over the rosemary and flaky salt. Bake at 230 °C for 22 minutes until deep golden, then leave it in the tin for five minutes before lifting it onto a rack so the base stays crisp.

### Soda bread

450 g plain flour, 1 teaspoon bicarbonate of soda, 1 teaspoon salt and 400 ml buttermilk, or 400 ml of milk soured with the juice of a lemon if there is no buttermilk in the house. A handful of oats on top is good but not needed. No yeast and no proving, so it is the bread to make when there is no time, and it is best eaten the same day.

Heat the oven first, with the tray in it, because the dough starts to rise as soon as the liquid meets the soda and should not sit around. Mix quickly with a fork into a soft, sticky dough; the less it is worked, the lighter it stays.

Shape it into a round on the hot, floured tray and cut a deep cross in the top, almost to the edges. Bake at 200 °C for 35 minutes; it is done when the base sounds hollow when tapped. Wrap it in a clean tea towel while it cools so the crust stays soft.

## Soups

### Lentil soup

200 g red lentils, 1 onion, 2 carrots, 2 cloves of garlic, 1 teaspoon ground cumin, 1.2 litres vegetable stock and the juice of half a lemon. Serves four.

Soften the onion and carrots in a little oil for ten minutes, add the garlic and the spice for one minute more, then the lentils and the stock. Simmer for 25 minutes, until the lentils have collapsed, and blend half of it so it is thick but not smooth. Add the lemon juice at the end, off the heat.

### Roasted tomato soup

1 kg ripe tomatoes, 1 red pepper, 1 onion, 4 cloves of garlic, 500 ml stock and a handful of basil. Halve the tomatoes and roast everything except the stock and basil at 200 °C for 40 minutes, until the edges catch. Blend with the stock and basil, and loosen with a splash of water if it is too thick.

## Cakes

### Lemon drizzle

225 g soft butter, 225 g caster sugar, 4 eggs, 225 g self-raising flour and the zest of 2 lemons; for the drizzle, the juice of those lemons and 85 g of granulated sugar. Everything should be at room temperature, the eggs especially, or the mixture curdles when they go in.

Beat the butter and sugar together for a good five minutes, until pale and fluffy. Add the eggs one at a time with a spoonful of the flour each, so it does not split, then fold in the rest of the flour and the zest with a large metal spoon, keeping as much air in as possible.

Scrape it into a lined loaf tin and level the top. Bake at 180 °C for 45 to 50 minutes, until a skewer comes out clean. Prick it all over while it is still hot and spoon the juice and sugar over it, so it sets into a crunchy crust as it cools in the tin.

### Banana bread

3 very ripe bananas, the blacker the better, 75 g melted butter, 150 g light brown sugar, 1 egg, 1 teaspoon baking soda, a pinch of salt and 190 g plain flour. A handful of chopped walnuts or dark chocolate is a good extra; stir it in at the very end.

Mash the bananas with a fork, leaving a few lumps, and beat in the melted butter. Stir in the sugar, the egg, the soda and the salt, then the flour, just until no dry flour shows. Overmixing makes it tough and gives it tunnels.

Pour it into a buttered loaf tin. Bake at 175 °C for an hour, covering it loosely with foil for the last 15 minutes if the top browns too fast, and let it cool for ten minutes before turning it out.
